Venetian Works of Defence on the Adriatic

A route through the components of the UNESCO transnational serial site Stato da Terra – Western Stato da Mar.

Solid pins mark the six UNESCO-inscribed components. Lighter pins mark intermediate stops along the route. Click any city to view photos.

In 2017, UNESCO inscribed Venetian Works of Defence between the 16th and 17th Centuries: Stato da Terra – Western Stato da Mar as a transnational serial property comprising six fortified sites built by the Republic of Venice in Italy, Croatia, and Montenegro. The components illustrate the bastioned alla moderna fortification system that spread across Europe in response to gunpowder artillery, and trace the Venetian defensive network from the western terraferma to the eastern Adriatic Stato da Mar.

The Stato da Terra cluster comprises the Venetian walls of Bergamo, the planned star-fortress town of Palmanova (founded 1593), and the lakeside stronghold of Peschiera del Garda. The Western Stato da Mar cluster continues along the Adriatic with the urban defences of Zadar, the St. Nicholas Fortress at the entrance to the Šibenik channel, and the fortifications of Kotor that climb from the bay to the ridge of St. John.

The dotted route above connects the six inscribed sites along the Adriatic spine, with intermediate stops at Podgorica, Ulcinj, Dubrovnik, Korčula, Split, Rijeka, and Trieste filling in the geography between them.
